# Verified

## Productive failure

**Sinha, T. & Kapur, M. (2021). When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction
Works: Evidence for Productive Failure.** *Review of Educational Research*,
91(5), 761–798.
53 studies, 166 comparisons. Hedges' g = 0.36 [0.20, 0.51] favouring
problem-solving-first, rising to 0.37–0.58 when implemented with high fidelity to
productive failure principles. Effects favoured instruction-first for second-to-
fifth graders and for domain-general skills.
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105
- https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1308129 (abstract, free)
- https://janfasen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Sinha-and-Kapur-PS-I.pdf (full PDF, free)

**Kapur, M. & Roll, I. — Productive Failure (overview chapter).**
Readable summary of the mechanism and the meta-analytic findings, including the
role of the consolidation phase.
- https://boldscience.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Productive-Failure.pdf

**Plain-language summaries** — useful for the writeup, not for citing.
- https://phys.org/news/2021-09-productively-wiser.html (ETH Zurich)
- https://www.weforum.org/stories/education-and-skills/students-who-productively-fail-learn-more/

## Inoculation and prebunking

**Basol, M., Roozenbeek, J. & van der Linden, S. (2020). Good News about Bad
News: Gamified Inoculation Boosts Confidence and Cognitive Immunity Against Fake
News.** *Journal of Cognition*, 3(1). Open access.
N = 196 with a gamified control group. Improved ability to spot manipulation
techniques and increased confidence in own judgments — confidence gains occurring
only for those who updated in the correct direction.
- https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.91
- DOI: 10.5334/joc.91

**The Bad News game itself** — play it before designing your exhibits.
- https://www.getbadnews.com
- Lab page with the full publication list: https://www.sdmlab.psychol.cam.ac.uk/research/bad-news-game

**Roozenbeek, J., van der Linden, S. & Nygren, T. (2020). Prebunking
interventions based on "inoculation" theory can reduce susceptibility to
misinformation across cultures.** *HKS Misinformation Review*, 1(2).
German, Greek, Polish, Swedish; n = 5,061.
- https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/global-vaccination-badnews/

**van der Linden, S., Roozenbeek, J. & Compton, J. (2020). Inoculating Against
Fake News About COVID-19.** *Frontiers in Psychology*, 11:566790. Open access.
Good short explanation of active vs passive inoculation and the "broad-spectrum"
technique-level approach — which is the mechanism our app copies.
- https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.566790/full

## Dark patterns — taxonomy

**Mathur, A., Acar, G., Friedman, M. J., Lucherini, E., Mayer, J., Chetty, M. &

- DOI: 10.1145/3411764.3445610

**Brignull, H. et al. — deceptive.design.**

## Dark patterns — user experience

**Gray, C. M., Chen, J., Chivukula, S. S. & Qu, L. (2021). End User Accounts of
Dark Patterns as Felt Manipulation.** *Proc. ACM Hum.-Comput. Interact.* 5,
CSCW2, Article 372.
Survey n = 169 in English and Mandarin plus nine interviews. Source of the
"broad awareness that something is off, but lacking precise language" finding,
and of the emotion set (distressed, upset, guilty, scared, hostile, irritable,
ashamed, nervous, jittery, afraid). Note surprise is absent from that set.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11046 (free)
- https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3479516
- Plain summary: https://deceptive.design/articles/end-user-accounts-of-dark-patterns-as-felt-manipulation/

**Bongard-Blanchy, K., Rossi, A., Rivas, S., Doublet, S., Koenig, V. & Lenzini,
G. (2021). "I am definitely manipulated, even when I am aware of it. It's
ridiculous!" — Dark Patterns from the End-User Perspective.** *DIS '21*,
763–776.
n = 406. Awareness does not equip users to oppose manipulative influence; higher
ability to discern is positively related to capacity to self-protect. The main
threat to any consumer-protection framing (supports D-015).
- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12653 (free)
- DOI: 10.1145/3461778.3462086

**Maier, M. & Harr, R. (2020). Dark Design Patterns: An End-User Perspective.**
*Human Technology*, 16(2), 170–199.
Source of the four-stage model: impression → assessment → balance →
acceptability.

**Di Geronimo, L., Braz, L., Fregnan, E., Palomba, F. & Bacchelli, A. (2020). UI
Dark Patterns and Where to Find Them: A Study on Mobile Applications and User
Perception.** *CHI '20*.
Mobile-specific rather than web — directly relevant to the native-medium
exhibits, and worth reading before writing E-03.
- DOI: 10.1145/3313831.3376600


## Challenges to the method — verified

These are the cards that threaten our own premise. Cite them yourself.

**Wang, S. Y. N., Phillips, S. C., Carley, K. M., Lin, H. & Pennycook, G. (2025).
Limited effectiveness of psychological inoculation against misinformation in a
social media feed.** *PNAS Nexus*, 4(6), pgaf172. Open access.
Five preregistered studies. Inoculation only decreased engagement when the feed
contained solely synthetic content relevant to the task; adding real tweets, or
synthetic tweets using other manipulation techniques, nullified the effect.
→ Sharper than "recognition doesn't transfer to behaviour": single-technique
training collapses in mixed-technique environments. Real interfaces stack
patterns; our exhibits teach one at a time.
- https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/6/pgaf172/8151956
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12134459/ (free)
- DOI: 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf172

**Modirrousta-Galian, A. & Higham, P. A. (2023).** Meta-analysis in *Journal of
Experimental Psychology: General*, 152, 2411–2437.
Found that Bad News primarily produces a **conservative response bias** rather
than improving discrimination — players become more suspicious of everything, not
better at telling true from false.
→ The most serious challenge on this list. Our analogous failure is a user who
finishes three exhibits distrusting all interfaces rather than identifying
specific patterns. Testable on day 5 by including an honest interface in the test
set and watching for false positives.

**Seabrooke, T., Modirrousta-Galian, A. & Higham, P. A. (2025). Re-examining the
bad news game: No evidence of improved discrimination of Indian true and fake
news headlines.** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*.
Preregistered, N = 150 Indian nationals via Prolific, counterbalanced. No
significant pre/post differences in discrimination or response bias when
collapsing across conditions. Failed to replicate Iyengar et al. (2023, *Applied
Cognitive Psychology* 37, 290–303).
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-025-02827-x
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12705795/ (free)
- DOI: 10.3758/s13423-025-02827-x

**Counterweights — the literature is contested, not settled against inoculation:**
- McPhedran, R. et al. (2023). Psychological inoculation protects against the
  social media infodemic. *Scientific Reports*, 13, 5780.
- Smith, F., Simchon, A., Holford, D. & Lewandowsky, S. (2025). Inoculation
  reduces social media engagement with affectively polarized content in the UK
  and US. *Communications Psychology*, 3, 11.
- Axelsson, C.-A. W., Nygren, T., Roozenbeek, J. & van der Linden, S. (2024). Bad
  news in the civics classroom: How serious gameplay fosters teenagers' ability
  to discern misinformation techniques. *Journal of Research on Technology in
  Education*. → Closest to our context: learners in an educational setting.

## Dark patterns — knowledge changes behaviour, verified

**Naheyan, T. & Oyibo, K. (2024). The Effect of Dark Patterns and User Knowledge
on User Experience and Decision-Making.** PERSUASIVE 2024, LNCS vol. 14636,
190–206.
Confirmshaming: 68% of those without dark-pattern knowledge chose the
vendor-promoted expensive plan, versus 35% of those with knowledge. Trick
question: 40% of knowledgeable users edited their initial choice, against 11% and
6% in the confirmshaming and control conditions.
- https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-58226-4_15
- DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-58226-4_15

**Oyibo, K. (2025). The influence of user knowledge and usage behaviour on
decision-making and perceived reputation of streaming sites that use dark
patterns.** *Behaviour & Information Technology*.
Extended journal version. Adds that users perceived both patterns as manipulative
without that damaging perceived trustworthiness or credibility of the site — a
useful, uncomfortable finding.
- https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2024.2447475
- DOI: 10.1080/0144929X.2024.2447475

# Cited but not link-verified

Referenced in the cards from training knowledge. Citation details should be
correct; confirm before formal use.

**McGuire, W. J. & Papageorgis, D. (1961).** The origin of inoculation theory.
The 1961 work is the standard citation; McGuire (1964) is also commonly cited.

**Barnett, S. M. & Ceci, S. J. (2002). When and where do we apply what we learn?
A taxonomy for far transfer.** *Psychological Bulletin*, 128(4), 612–637.
The transfer-distance framework behind the varied-surface-features requirement.

**Bjork, R. A. — desirable difficulties.** Multiple papers across the 1990s and
2000s; Bjork & Bjork (2011) is the usual accessible citation.

**Marton, F. — variation theory.** *Necessary Conditions of Learning* (2015) is
the book-length treatment; earlier papers with Booth (1997).

**Maertens, R., Roozenbeek, J., Basol, M. & van der Linden, S. (2020/2021).
Long-term effectiveness of inoculation against misinformation: Three
longitudinal experiments.** *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*,
27(1), 1–16. Source of the 13-week-with-boosters finding.

**Maertens, R. et al. (2025). Psychological booster shots targeting memory
increase long-term resistance against misinformation.** *Nature Communications*.
Listed on the Cambridge lab page; the most recent word on durability.

**Roozenbeek, J., Traberg, C. S. & van der Linden, S. (2022). Technique-based
inoculation against real-world misinformation.** *Royal Society Open Science*, 9,
